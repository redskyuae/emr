# Clinical Entity Forms Use Dedicated Pages, Not Sheets

Every CRUD surface built so far — Users, Roles, Assets, Appointment Masters — creates and edits records in a slide-over Sheet whose open-state lives in the URL via `nuqs` (`docs/adr/0010`). Patient Registration is the first clinical entity form, and it is a different shape of work: roughly twenty-five fields across five sections (demographics, contact, address, identifiers, emergency contact), with dependent Country → State selection and five Global Reference dropdowns. Squeezing that into a slide-over produces a long, cramped scroll for what is a primary front-desk workflow, not a quick configuration tweak. The app shell had in fact already anticipated this: the Patients primary action pointed at `/patients/new` before the page existed.

We are giving clinical entity forms dedicated routes: `/patients/new` for Patient Registration, `/patients/[id]/edit` for updates, and a read-only `/patients/[id]` detail view as the row-click destination and the future anchor for Appointments, Visits, and Admissions to link to. The form is a single scrolling page of grouped section cards with one submit — not a wizard — so front-desk entry stays fast and `react-hook-form` server-error mapping via `setError` lands on a visible field rather than a hidden step.

This does not weaken ADR 0010. Sheets driven by a `nuqs` param remain the pattern for configuration and master-data CRUD, where the form is small and staying in list context is the point. The dividing line is the nature of the entity: records a hospital operates _on_ (config, masters, access) stay in sheets; records the hospital's work is _about_ (Patients, and later Admissions and similar clinical forms) get pages, because their forms are large, their records need a durable read view, and their URLs become link targets for other modules.

The trade-off is a heavier build per module — three routes with loaders instead of one list page with a sheet — and two conventions for contributors to choose between instead of one. We accept that because the alternative (one-size sheets) was already visibly failing at Patient scale, and the page/sheet split gives future clinical modules a rule to apply rather than a judgment call to re-litigate. Reversing this means re-plumbing routes that other modules will link to, so it should be treated as settled for clinical entities.
