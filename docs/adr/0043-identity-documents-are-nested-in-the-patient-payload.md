# Identity Documents Are Nested In The Patient Payload, Not A Sub-Resource

An Identity Document is a government-issued document evidencing a Patient's identity — passport, residence visa, national identity card, driving licence — recorded with the details that make it meaningful, chiefly the issuing country and the expiry date. A Patient may hold any number of them, including several of the same kind. They are stored in `patient_identity_document` and exposed **nested inside the Patient contract**: returned as an array on the Patient read models, and replaced wholesale by `POST /api/v1/patients` and `PUT /api/v1/patients/[id]`. There is no `/api/v1/patients/[id]/identity-documents` endpoint.

This deliberately breaks a visible pattern. Every other Patient sub-collection — Allergies, Medications, Problems, Vital Signs, Clinical Notes — is a sub-resource with its own route and its own sheet under `_chart/_sheets/`. A reader who finds Identity Documents modelled differently will reasonably assume it was an oversight and try to "fix" it.

The pattern does not fit, for two reasons. The first is that **the sub-resource shape cannot serve registration at all.** You cannot `POST /patients/123/identity-documents` while registering a brand-new patient, because there is no `123` yet — and a passport in the receptionist's hand is exactly what is being recorded at that moment. Sub-resources would force registration into two calls, where the second failing on a flaky connection leaves a patient created with no documents and a form with no idea how to recover.

The second is that the two kinds of collection are not the same kind of data. Chart records are **clinical events**: added one at a time, later, by different clinicians, each carrying independent significance and its own audit trail. Identity Documents are **registration demographics** — captured alongside the name and the phone number, on one form, in one save. They are nearer to `addressLine1` than to an allergy. Nesting them also matches what `PUT /patients/[id]` already does, since `updatePatientSchema` is already a full replace of the whole Patient; making one sub-array the exception would be the inconsistency.

The cost is accepted rather than unnoticed. Full-replace clobbers concurrent edits — two receptionists on the same Patient, last write wins on the entire document list — and there is no per-document record of who added or removed what. For registration data edited rarely, by one person at a desk, that is a fair trade. Sustained demand for document provenance is the signal to promote them to a sub-resource.

**The replace is diffed by document ID rather than rewritten.** The naive implementation of "replace the array" — soft-delete every existing row, insert every incoming row — satisfies ADR 0012 and is quietly corrosive: editing a Patient's phone number twenty times tombstones and reinserts their unchanged passport twenty times, leaving nineteen meaningless dead rows and a `createdOn` that permanently reads "today". So each document carries its `id` in both directions. The command updates matched rows, inserts rows arriving without an `id`, and soft-deletes only those actually absent from the payload — one tombstone per genuine removal, and `createdOn` that means what it says. Those tombstones also partially recover the audit trail given up above: a removed passport leaves a trace that it was once held.

Accepting client-supplied row IDs creates an obvious hole, and closing it is not optional: the validator must confirm every submitted `id` belongs to _that_ Patient within _that_ Tenant before the command touches it. Without that check, `PUT /patients/12` carrying a document ID owned by a patient in another Tenant rewrites their data — a cross-tenant write through an identifier nobody thought to validate.
